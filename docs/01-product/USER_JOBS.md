# User Jobs / JTBD

Status: **P2 WORKING DRAFT — not yet active for approval until P1 wedge is approved**

Updated: 2026-09-15

## Purpose

Describe what the user is trying to accomplish in their own terms before we decide screens, navigation or technical objects.

The jobs below are hypotheses distilled from the relaunch research and prior MetaEdge usage. They must be validated against the approved V1 wedge.

## Primary job

> **When I notice something that might be an edge, help me understand whether it deserves attention, test it safely, and keep managing it without losing the original reasoning.**

## Job map

### J-A — Notice what deserves attention

User thought:

> “There is too much happening. Show me what is worth looking at now and why.”

Success means:

- the user sees a manageable set of potentially relevant opportunities/sources;
- urgency and importance are distinguishable;
- nothing is presented as guaranteed or “safe” merely because it ranks highly;
- the user can immediately inspect the reason something surfaced.

### J-B — Understand before trusting

User thought:

> “What actually happened? Why might it matter? What do we know, infer and not know?”

Success means:

- the user can separate facts from interpretation;
- supportive and conflicting evidence are visible;
- data/source freshness and incompleteness are understandable;
- wallet/trader behavior is not falsely represented as complete intent.

### J-C — Decide how deeply to engage

User thought:

> “This looks interesting. What are my options besides either doing nothing or trading it?”

Success means the user can choose an appropriate next relationship such as:

- dismiss/ignore;
- save/follow;
- compare;
- shadow;
- backtest where appropriate;
- create/adapt a strategy;
- paper try;
- delegate bounded paper monitoring/action.

These choices must feel meaningfully different.

### J-D — Test without pretending uncertainty is gone

User thought:

> “Let me find out whether this idea/source deserves trust without betting real capital.”

Success means:

- assumptions are visible;
- paper/shadow modes are unmistakable;
- the user understands what is being tested;
- weak evidence can produce a small experiment when the chosen method allows it;
- one loss does not automatically invalidate a good process.

### J-E — Know what is happening after entry

User thought:

> “I already have exposure. Has anything changed, and do I need to do something?”

Success means:

- the product does not stop at entry;
- the user sees what changed in evidence/risk/position;
- additions, reductions, holds, exits or reversals are explained;
- the user can understand what the agent is monitoring next.

### J-F — Learn whether the decision process deserved trust

User thought:

> “Was that a good decision, or did I just get lucky/unlucky?”

Success means:

- outcome quality and decision quality are separated;
- the original decision-time information is preserved;
- missed opportunities and good abstentions can both be reviewed;
- the user can see which source/strategy/agent contributed to the result.

### J-G — Build trust progressively

User thought:

> “I want more automation only after I have evidence that this process behaves well.”

Success means:

- authority is progressive and explicit;
- watch/follow/shadow/paper-agent/real states cannot be confused;
- the user can increase, reduce or revoke authority;
- the product can explain why an agent has the authority it has.

## Secondary jobs

### Learn

> “Teach me what is happening without making me learn an institutional trading terminal first.”

### Compare sources

> “Which wallets, traders, strategies or agents are worth studying for my objective and risk tolerance?”

### Express an idea

> “I have a thesis. Help me turn it into something explicit enough to test and review.”

### Copy intelligently

> “I like what this source does, but adapt it to my capital and risk rather than blindly cloning it.”

### Run experiments in parallel

> “Let several ideas/agents operate without losing account-level coherence.”

## Anti-jobs

V1 should not make the user feel responsible for:

- assembling many disconnected dashboards;
- translating every signal into a strategy from scratch;
- reasoning about internal state-machine names;
- manually remembering why an action was taken;
- interpreting a single opaque confidence score;
- deciding whether a pending execution can safely be retried;
- distinguishing paper from real by subtle UI cues.

## P2 exit criterion

After P1 is approved, review this job map and answer:

- Which 3–5 jobs are truly primary for V1?
- Which jobs are supporting rather than separate product surfaces?
- What would make a user say each job is complete?
- Which jobs can be deferred without breaking the master experience loop?
